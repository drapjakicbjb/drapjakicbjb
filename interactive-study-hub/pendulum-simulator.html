Physics of Simple Harmonic Motion: The Simple Pendulum

A simple pendulum consists of a point mass (the bob) suspended from a frictionless pivot by a light, inextensible string of length L. When displaced from its central equilibrium position by a small initial angle θ and released, gravitational force provides a restoring torque that pulls the pendulum bob back toward equilibrium, initiating periodic oscillatory motion known as Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM).

1. Key Equations Governing Pendulum Motion

Period of Oscillation (T)
SHM Dynamics
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \quad \text{and} \quad f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{g}{L}}$$
\(T\) = Time Period in seconds (\(\text{s}\))
\(L\) = String Length in meters (\(\text{m}\))
\(g\) = Gravity acceleration (\(9.8\text{ m/s}^2\))
\(f\) = Frequency in Hertz (\(\text{Hz}\))
Step-by-Step Worked Example:
For a pendulum with length \(L = 10\text{ m}\) under Earth gravity (\(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)): $$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{10}{9.8}} \approx 6.283 \times 1.010 = 6.346\text{ s}$$ Notice that period \(T\) depends ONLY on length \(L\) and gravity \(g\)—bob mass \(m\) plays zero role!
Conservation of Mechanical Energy
Energy Balance
$$E_{\text{total}} = PE + KE = mgh + \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \text{constant}$$
\(PE\) = Potential Energy (\(mgh\)) in Joules
\(KE\) = Kinetic Energy (\(\frac{1}{2}mv^2\)) in Joules
\(m\) = Bob Mass in kg
\(h\) = Height above minimum point
Calculation:
At maximum height (\(h_{\text{max}}\)), \(KE = 0 \implies E_{\text{total}} = mgh_{\text{max}}\). As the bob passes through vertical equilibrium (\(h = 0\)), \(PE = 0 \implies v_{\text{max}} = \sqrt{2gh_{\text{max}}}\).
  • Independence of Mass: Notice that mass (m) does NOT appear in the period equation. Provided air resistance is neglected, a 1 kg bob and a 50 kg bob oscillate with the exact same period.
  • Conservation of Mechanical Energy: At maximum angular displacement (amplitude), velocity is zero and mechanical energy exists entirely as Gravitational Potential Energy (PE = mgh). As the bob swings through the bottom equilibrium point, height h reaches zero and potential energy converts completely into Kinetic Energy (KE = ½ mv²).
  • Total Mechanical Energy: \(E_{total} = PE + KE = \text{constant}\).

2. Interactive Lab Experiments to Try

  1. Varying Length: Double the string length (L) from 5m to 10m and observe how period length increases by a factor of \(\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414\).
  2. Extraterrestrial Gravity: Lower gravity (g) to 1.6 m/s² (Moon's gravity) to see how slow pendulum swings become in low-gravity environments.
  3. Real-Time Energy Bar: Monitor the PE vs KE bar chart under the simulator as potential energy peaks at the ends and kinetic energy peaks at the vertical midpoint.

3. Frequently Asked Questions (Physics & SHM)

Q1: Why does the small-angle approximation (sin θ ≈ θ) apply to simple pendulums?

For angles less than 15° (approx. 0.26 radians), sin(θ) differs from θ in radians by less than 1%. This linearity makes the restoring force directly proportional to displacement, fulfilling the strict criteria for pure Simple Harmonic Motion.

Q2: What is a Foucault Pendulum?

A Foucault Pendulum is a large, free-swinging pendulum designed to demonstrate the Earth's diurnal rotation. As Earth rotates beneath the pendulum, the plane of oscillation appears to rotate slowly over several hours.
