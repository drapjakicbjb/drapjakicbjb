Physics of Spherical Mirrors: Laws of Reflection & Image Formation

A spherical mirror is a curved reflecting surface cut from a hollow glass sphere. When the inner surface is polished and reflecting, it forms a Concave (Converging) Mirror. When the outer bulging surface is reflecting, it forms a Convex (Diverging) Mirror. Spherical mirrors follow the fundamental Laws of Reflection (\(\text{Angle of Incidence } i = \text{Angle of Reflection } r\)).

1. The Mirror Formula & Linear Magnification

The Mirror Equation
Spherical Reflection
$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} \quad \text{where } f = \frac{R}{2}$$
\(f\) = Focal Length measured from Pole (\(P\))
\(v\) = Image Distance from Pole
\(u\) = Object Distance from Pole
\(R\) = Radius of Curvature
Step-by-Step Worked Example:
For a Concave Mirror with focal length \(f = -1.00\text{ m}\) (\(-100\text{ cm}\)) and an object at \(u = -1.50\text{ m}\) (\(-150\text{ cm}\)): $$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{-1.00} - \frac{1}{-1.50} = \frac{-3 + 2}{3.00} = -\frac{1}{3.00}$$ $$\implies v = -3.00\text{ m}$$ The negative sign indicates a Real, Inverted Image formed \(3.00\text{ m}\) in front of the mirror.
Mirror Linear Magnification
Image Scaling
$$m = -\frac{v}{u} = \frac{h'}{h}$$
\(m\) = Linear Magnification
\(h'\) = Image Height
\(h\) = Object Height
Sign Rule: \(m < 0\) (Inverted/Real), \(m > 0\) (Erect/Virtual)
Calculation:
$$m = -\left(\frac{-3.00}{-1.50}\right) = -2.000 \implies h' = -2.000 \times 0.50\text{ m} = -1.00\text{ m}$$ Magnification \(m = -2.000\) shows the image is inverted and doubled in height.

2. Practical Applications of Spherical Mirrors

  • Concave Mirrors: Used in astronomical reflecting telescopes, solar concentrators, dentist headlamps, and shaving mirrors (to produce magnified virtual images when object is inside focus).
  • Convex Mirrors: Used as vehicle rear-view mirrors and blind corner security mirrors because they always produce an upright, diminished virtual image with a wide field of view.

3. Frequently Asked Questions (Mirror Optics)

Q1: Why is focal length negative for concave mirrors and positive for convex mirrors under sign convention?

Under the New Cartesian Sign Convention, distances measured against the direction of incident light (in front of mirror) are negative. Since a concave mirror's focus lies in front of the mirror, its focal length is negative. A convex mirror's focus lies behind the mirror, making its focal length positive.
