Human Cardiovascular Physiology: Heart Chambers, Cardiac Cycle & Blood Circulation

The human heart is a hollow, muscular organ located in the thoracic cavity within the mediastinum. Operating continuously as a double-action pressure pump, the heart beats approximately 100,000 times per day, pumping over 7,200 liters of blood through an extensive network of blood vessels to deliver oxygen and nutrients to tissues while removing metabolic waste products.

1. Four Chambers & Atrioventricular Valves

  • Right Atrium (RA): Receives deoxygenated systemic venous return from the Superior Vena Cava (SVC) and Inferior Vena Cava (IVC).
  • Right Ventricle (RV): Pumps deoxygenated blood through the Tricuspid Valve and Pulmonary Semilunar Valve into the Pulmonary Artery leading to the lungs.
  • Left Atrium (LA): Receives oxygen-rich blood returned from the lungs via four Pulmonary Veins.
  • Left Ventricle (LV): Thick-walled muscular chamber that contracts forcefully to pump oxygenated blood through the Mitral (Bicuspid) Valve and Aortic Semilunar Valve into the systemic Aorta.

2. Phases of the Cardiac Cycle & Electrical Conduction System

The rhythmic contraction of the heart is driven by an intrinsic electrical conduction system operating in synchronized sequence:

  • Sinoatrial (SA) Node Pacemaker: Located in the right atrium wall, the SA node generates spontaneous electrical action potentials at 60–100 beats per minute, causing both atria to contract (Atrial Systole).
  • Atrioventricular (AV) Node Delay: The impulse pauses briefly (~0.1 seconds) at the AV node to allow full ventricular filling before ventricular contraction begins.
  • Bundle of His & Purkinje Fibers: Action potentials travel rapidly down the interventricular septum through the left and right bundle branches, spreading through Purkinje fibers to trigger coordinated bottom-up Ventricular Systole.
  • Systole vs. Diastole: During Systole, the ventricles contract forcefully to eject blood into the aorta and pulmonary artery. During Diastole, the heart muscle relaxes, allowing the chambers to fill with blood for the next cardiac cycle.

3. Frequently Asked Questions (Cardiology)

Q1: What causes the characteristic 'LUB-DUB' heart sounds heard through a stethoscope?

The first heart sound ('LUB', S1) is produced by the closure of the Atrioventricular (Tricuspid and Mitral) valves at the onset of ventricular systole. The second heart sound ('DUB', S2) is produced by the sudden closure of the Semilunar (Aortic and Pulmonary) valves at the beginning of ventricular diastole.

Q2: What is the primary natural pacemaker of the heart?

The Sinoatrial (SA) Node, located in the upper wall of the right atrium, generates spontaneous electrical impulses (action potentials) that spread rapidly across the atrial myocardium to initiate contraction.
