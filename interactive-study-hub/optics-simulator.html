Fundamentals of Geometrical Optics: Thin Lens Refraction & Ray Tracing

Geometrical optics studies the propagation of light in terms of rays. When light passes from one transparent optical medium (such as air) into a spherical glass lens, it undergoes refraction (bending) at both spherical interfaces according to Snell's Law (\(n_1 \sin \theta_1 = n_2 \sin \theta_2\)). The geometry of thin lenses allows us to predict image formation using fundamental optical equations and Cartesian sign conventions.

1. The Thin Lens Formula & Sign Conventions

The Thin Lens Equation
Gaussian Optics
$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u}$$
\(f\) = Focal Length (cm / m)
\(v\) = Image Distance from Optical Center
\(u\) = Object Distance from Optical Center
Sign Convention: Convex (\(f > 0\)), Concave (\(f < 0\))
Step-by-Step Worked Example:
Given a Convex Lens with focal length \(f = +1.00\text{ m}\) (\(+100\text{ cm}\)) and an object placed at distance \(u = -1.50\text{ m}\) (\(-150\text{ cm}\)): $$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{1.00} + \frac{1}{-1.50} = \frac{3 - 2}{3.00} = \frac{1}{3.00}$$ $$\implies v = +3.00\text{ m}$$ The positive sign of \(v\) indicates a Real, Inverted Image formed at \(3.00\text{ m}\) behind the lens.
Linear Magnification & Dioptric Lens Power
Optical Ratios
$$m = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{h'}{h} \qquad \text{and} \qquad P = \frac{1}{f\text{ (m)}}$$
\(m\) = Linear Magnification
\(h'\) = Image Height
\(h\) = Object Height
\(P\) = Dioptric Power in Diopters (\(\text{D}\))
Calculation:
Using values from above (\(v = +3.00\text{ m}\), \(u = -1.50\text{ m}\), object height \(h = 0.50\text{ m}\)): $$m = \frac{+3.00}{-1.50} = -2.000 \implies h' = -2.000 \times 0.50\text{ m} = -1.00\text{ m}$$ The magnification \(m = -2.000\) confirms an inverted image twice the size of the object.

2. Three Principal Rays for Image Construction

  • Parallel Ray: A ray incident parallel to the principal axis refracts through the principal focus (\(F_2\)) on the opposite side of a convex lens (or appears to diverge from \(F_1\) in a concave lens).
  • Optical Center Ray: A ray passing directly through the optical center (\(O\)) passes straight through without suffering any net angular deviation.
  • Focal Ray: A ray passing through the first principal focus (\(F_1\)) emerges parallel to the principal axis after refraction.

4. Frequently Asked Questions (Optics & Lenses)

Q1: What is Dioptric Power of a Lens?

Power (\(P\)) is defined as the reciprocal of focal length expressed in meters (\(P = 1/f\)). The SI unit of lens power is the Diopter (\(D\)). Convex lenses have positive power, while concave lenses have negative power.

Q2: What is the key difference between a real image and a virtual image?

A real image is formed by actual intersection of light rays and can be projected onto a screen. A virtual image is formed when light rays only appear to diverge from a focal point, and it cannot be caught on a physical screen.
